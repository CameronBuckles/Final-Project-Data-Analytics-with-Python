# Final-Project-Data-Analytics-with-Python
The purpose of this analysis was to determine what contributing factors apply when determining what makes a Dragon Ball Z character powerful, and whether or not a character's race is associated with their Power Level in the Dragon Ball Z dataset.
We analyzed the original dataset information and found that there were missing values. We then dropped the missing values while preserving character information even when some information was unavailable. We also removed exact duplicate records so characters aren't counted more than once in our analysis. In data manipulation, we sorted Power Level from highest to lowest and found that the two most powerful characters in the dataset belonged to the Angel race, and the third most powerful character was an Alien. The average power level ranges from Angels at 200,000 and next, the Aliens at 40,500, to Shape-shifters and Grasshoppers coming in at below the average of 10.
There is a discrepancy in the dataset as seen in the Frequently Appearing Abilities visualization that shows that the most common ability is Android Strikes. This is a discrepancy because androids have an average power level of 7,680 which is far below an Angel or Alien. That character race has the most count of independent and unique attacks but they are not the most powerful. We see in the Power Level vs Speed visualization that slower characters tend to be weaker and that the most powerful characters are also the fastest. The Average Power Level and Character Count by Race visualization shows us just how high above the average an Angel’s power level is in comparison to the dataset. It also shows that there is no direct correlation between count of character race and their respective power levels.
I used the correlation ratio to measure the strength of the relationship between Race and Power Level because Race is a categorical variable and Power Level is numerical. I also performed an ANOVA test to determine whether the average Power Levels differed significantly between Races. The results showed an Eta value of 0.903, indicating a very strong association between race and Power Level. The p-value was approximately 3.87 × 10⁻¹⁴, which is significantly lower than the 0.05 significance level. Therefore I rejected the null hypothesis and concluded that there is strong statistical evidence that Power Levels differ between character races.
The Eta-squared value of 0.816 indicates that approximately 81.6% of the variation in Power Level is associated with differences between the race groups in this dataset. Overall, this analysis suggests that race is strongly associated with character strength in Dragon Ball Z.
